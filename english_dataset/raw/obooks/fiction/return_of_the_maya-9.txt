The first thing Casey heard was the sound of helicopters. Then, he saw them, hundreds upon hundreds of armed vehicles of destruction pouring into the valley. There were tanks, armored cars, and jeeps. The smell of exhaust burned his nostrils, and he had to restrain himself from vomiting. *'Dad, what have you done?'* he wondered.

Then, as if reading his mind, his dad came over to him. “Casey, I think it will be best for you to stay in the temple. It will be the safest spot when the fighting begins.”

“Sure, Dad, whatever you say.” Casey was seething, but he didn’t let it show. He simply let the anger sit inside him and grow. He didn’t understand why his dad was being so unreasonable. He thought that they should at least let the aliens explain themselves. He went and sat in his hammock and thought about how he hated his life. Then, he heard something on the radio that caught his attention.

“*Just in from the Keck Observatory, it’s Edward Johnson. How are you Edward?*”

Casey didn’t know what was happening, because he didn’t have a television screen to watch the news. He only had the radio. He just assumed that Edward was talking. “*Pretty good, thanks Tom. Things are getting pretty hairy up here at the Keck. It seems that their astronomers have seen a sort of wormhole open up just outside the Earth’s atmosphere. Some speculate that this is the end of the world, but you need not fear, for we have everything under control. We do not know what this is yet, but we will report as soon as we find out. Now, back to you Tom.*”

“*Well, on a lighter note, children in South Africa are...*” Casey didn’t hear the rest. His mind had wandered to an unreachable state. He envisioned giant wormholes destroying the earth over and over again. As Casey snapped out of his reverie, night fell—or did it? He looked at his watch. It was only two o’clock. He went outside and looked up into the sky. What he saw there took his breath away.
