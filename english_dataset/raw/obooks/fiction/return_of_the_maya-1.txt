Casey watched as the sunlight filtered through the trees. To him it looked like honey trickling from a spoon. He hadn’t really wanted to come to the jungle with his father. James had insisted, though, so he didn’t really have much of a choice.  

Suddenly, the jeep he was riding in came to an abrupt stop. Roberto got out and announced, “This is where the road ends. From here we will have to walk.” Roberto had a heavy Spanish accent due to his childhood in Mexico. 

Casey didn’t really understand why they needed a Mexican guide, but his father had explained it was because he knew the land.  Casey got out and buckled his backpack on. It’s going to be a long walk, he thought. The dig site was about five kilometers from the trail head, so if they wanted to get there before it got dark, they would need to hurry. 

As if James could read his mind, he said, “We’d better get a move on; it will be dark soon, and anybody with any sense doesn’t want to be in the jungle at night.” They turned on their headlamps and began up the trail. 

The hike was long and uneventful until they reached the top of a large hill, almost a mountain. They rose up above the crest and looked down upon a valley blanketed in trees. Casey couldn’t believe it. Nestled in the folds of the Earth was a huge Mayan temple.
